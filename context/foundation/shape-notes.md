## Vision & Problem Statement

People with digestive complaints (stomach pain, heartburn, bloating, bowel issues) cannot reliably connect food to symptoms. Traditional food journals fail because entries are made retrospectively from memory, and wellbeing is tracked rarely and irregularly — leaving no reliable dataset to discuss with a doctor or dietitian. Patterns are invisible; there is nothing concrete to bring to a specialist.

**Key insight**: Timing is the innovation. Triggered check-ins at the right moment (1.5–3h post-meal, plus fixed morning and evening check-ins) capture symptoms before they are forgotten. Generic tracking apps (MyFitnessPal, notes apps) do not prompt at the right time; they require the user to remember to log retroactively.

**Pain type**: Data trapped in memory / scattered notes; communication gap with doctor or dietitian. (Open: whether decision paralysis — "which food to avoid" — is equally central.)

## User & Persona

**Primary persona**: The author — a single named user managing recurring digestive issues. MVP is a personal tool; no account system, no multi-user complexity, no onboarding flow.

The persona:
- Has recurring digestive complaints and suspects food triggers
- Wants reliable data to bring to a doctor or dietitian
- Is motivated to track consistently but needs timing prompts and a low-friction interface
- Does not need calorie counts, macros, or food photos — wants minimum viable friction

## Access Control

Auth model: email + password login (or magic link). Single account — the author. No registration flow required; the one account is seeded directly.

Role model: flat. No admin/member/guest separation. MVP has exactly one user; role-based access control is a deliberate non-goal.

## Success Criteria

### Primary
Over 2 weeks of continuous use, data completeness ≥ 70%: responses on scheduled/triggered check-ins vs. check-ins generated by the system.

### Secondary
Timeline view allows the user to find how she felt after a specific meal from several days ago in under 1 minute.

### Guardrails
- Every logged meal must have at least one temporally linked check-in within the system's tracking window.
- Adding a new client type (e.g., mobile app) must not require changes to backend domain logic — API is the single source of truth for all business rules.

## Business Logic

**Core rule**: The app decides when to ask about symptoms based on when a meal was logged — not based on clock time alone.

**Inputs**: the timestamp of a logged meal. 90 minutes after a meal is recorded, a post-meal check-in entry is placed in the pending queue.

**Output**: a check-in entry in the pending queue, visible to the user on next app open.

**Conflict rule (meals close together)**: if a second meal is logged before the first meal's 90-minute check-in has been answered, the user is asked whether they want to keep the first check-in (90 min after meal 1) or defer it (90 min after meal 2). If they keep it — it stays in queue as-is. If they defer — the first check-in is replaced by a single check-in due 90 min after the second meal.

**Quiet hours rule**: the user can configure a quiet hours window (e.g., 23:00–07:00). Post-meal check-ins that would fall within quiet hours are suppressed — not queued. Morning and evening fixed check-ins are not affected by quiet hours.

### US-01: Logging a meal and seeing a linked check-in
Given the user has logged into the app,
When they submit a meal entry (date, time, free-text description),

## Non-Functional Requirements

- **Response time**: any user-initiated interaction (meal log submit, check-in submit, timeline load) completes in under 1 second as perceived by the user. The app is used in-the-moment; lag breaks the tracking habit.
- **Privacy**: health data is stored server-side and accessible only via an authenticated session. No third-party analytics, no data sharing with external services, no passive telemetry.
- **Data portability**: all stored data (meals and check-ins) is exportable as CSV or JSON on user request, in a format usable in a spreadsheet or shareable with a doctor or dietitian. (See FR-009.)
- **Device support**: desktop browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari) for MVP. Mobile browser support deferred to v2.
- **No diagnostic output**: the app never surfaces a diagnosis, intolerance verdict, or causal claim. Raw co-occurrence patterns may be shown ("heartburn appeared on 4 of 5 days with meal containing X"), but framed as user-discoverable pattern, not system verdict.

## Non-Goals

- **No automatic analysis or diagnostic verdicts**: the app shows raw co-occurrence data only. No inference engine, no "you're probably intolerant to X." (Rationale: diagnostic claims require clinical validation; out of scope and potentially harmful without it.)
- **No mobile app (iOS/Android native)**: web browser only for MVP. Native mobile deferred to v2.
- **No multi-user accounts or onboarding flow**: MVP is single-user (the author). Registration, invitations, and team/shared features deferred.
- **No food database, calorie counting, macro tracking, or photo logging**: meal entry is free-text description only. Nutritional data is not a concern of this product.
- **No push notifications or background jobs**: trigger mechanism is pending-queue-on-login only. No email reminders, no service workers.
- **No Apple Watch or other wearable integration**: data entry is manual only for MVP.
- **No HealthKit / sleep / HRV / activity data integration**: scope is limited to self-reported meals and symptoms.
